# Never Drop the Thread — The Prompts

*The exact prompts from the live session. Run them on your own
relationship and your own tools, with your contract loaded, and notice
the gap the same way the room did.*

## Round 1 — no contract (what a chatbot does with this)

Fresh conversation. No Project, no instructions, no connections.

> Handle my follow-ups with [person].

Notice where it guesses, where it asks you to do its work, and what it
never touches: your calendar, the thread from three weeks ago, the
thing you promised.

## Round 2 — the agent (goal, then plan)

Inside your Project, with the contract as its instructions and your
inbox and calendar connected.

> Your goal is a relationship brief for [person] that tells me who is
> waiting on whom, what has been committed, and what should happen
> next, so I can act today without re-reading everything.
>
> Work in stages and show each one:
>
> 1. **Retrieve.** Find every calendar event with [person] from 14 days
>    ago to 14 days ahead, and every email thread with them from the
>    last 30 days. [Add: and Slack messages in the last 30 days.]
> 2. **Correlate.** Match meetings to the threads that led to them or
>    followed them. Build a dated timeline.
> 3. **Extract.** List every commitment, mine and theirs, quoting the
>    sentence it came from. Separate explicit commitments from
>    inferred next steps and label each one.
> 4. **Classify.** Flag unanswered questions, stalled decisions,
>    deadlines, and anything that looks late or misunderstood. Say why.
> 5. **Recommend.** One next action. Say what happens if I do nothing.
> 6. **Draft.** Write the follow-up in my voice, save it to Drafts,
>    and stop. Do not send anything.
>
> Attach evidence to every conclusion. Mark anything you are not sure
> about as uncertain rather than guessing. Then tell me what is left
> that needs my judgment.

Same tool. The only thing that changed is that the agent knows what it
is trying to accomplish and how you would break it down yourself.

## Round 3 — teach it one rule

After you have read the first brief and marked what it missed,
misunderstood, or over-inferred:

> Add this rule to how you work for me, and re-run the brief:
> [your rule, e.g. "A suggestion is not a commitment. Only count
> something as a commitment if one of us said we would do it, with a
> what and a when."]

Then compare the two briefs. If nothing changed, the rule was not
specific enough.

## Round 4 — what could run on a schedule

Once the brief is one you would act on without editing:

> If you ran this every morning for the three relationships that
> matter most this month, what would you check, what would you flag,
> and what would you never do without asking me first?

The answer is the spec for your first recurring agent. Set it up only
when you would trust it unattended, and keep "never send" in place
until you have a reason to loosen it.

## Where these run

- **Claude:** a Project with the contract as instructions and
  connectors granted to the Project.
- **ChatGPT:** a Project or Custom GPT with the contract as
  instructions; connectors depend on your plan.
- **Gemini:** a Gem with the trimmed contract; Google Workspace access
  through Extensions.

Agentic behavior, meaning the agent reading your real inbox and
calendar and acting in stages, needs a paid plan in every tool. The
contract, the brief structure, and the discipline of reading a first
result cold do not.
