# Relationship Brief — Template

*What the agent produces. Nine fields, every one of them with a
source. If a field has no evidence line, it is an assumption, and it
says so.*

| Field | Agent output |
|---|---|
| **Relationship** | Who this person is and why they matter to me right now |
| **Recent activity** | Meetings and message exchanges in scope, dated |
| **My commitments** | What I said I would do, each with the message or meeting it came from |
| **Their commitments** | What they agreed to do, each with its source |
| **Open questions** | Items asked and not answered, in either direction |
| **Risk** | What may be stalled, late, or misunderstood, and why the agent thinks so |
| **Recommended action** | The single next best step, and what would happen if I did nothing |
| **Evidence** | The email, meeting, or message that supports each conclusion above |
| **Draft** | A follow-up message for my approval. Not sent. |

## How to read your first brief

Read it cold, before you explain anything to yourself. Then mark each
line with one of four words:

- **Right.** It found what happened and labeled it correctly.
- **Missed.** Something in scope that it did not surface.
- **Misunderstood.** Right source, wrong reading.
- **Over-inferred.** Presented a guess as a fact, or a suggestion as a
  commitment.
- **Unsupported.** A conclusion with no evidence line. Treat it as an assumption.

Every "misunderstood" and "over-inferred" is a candidate for a
judgment rule in section 7 of your contract. That is how the agent
stops being a template.
