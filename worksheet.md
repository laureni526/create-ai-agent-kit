# Sketch Your First Skill

*Ten minutes, no tool required. Fill this in whether you built along
in the session or you're starting fresh right now.*

Four questions. Answer them in order — each one narrows the last.

---

## 1. One thing people ask me to explain over and over

Not the impressive thing. The thing you're *tired* of re-explaining —
the question that lands in your inbox, your DMs, or a meeting so often
you could recite your own answer in your sleep.

> ___

**If you're stuck:** think about the last time someone on your team,
in your community, or in your inbox asked you something you'd already
answered for someone else that same month.

---

## 2. The task I'd want an agent to do with that knowledge

Not "understand my whole job." One specific, bounded task where having
that knowledge already loaded would change the output.

> ___

**Test it:** could you hand this task to a smart new hire who'd read
your answer to Question 1 and nothing else? If yes, you've scoped it
right.

---

## 3. The one tool it would need to reach to actually do it

Most tasks don't need a connection at all — a skill by itself already
helps. But if this task requires the agent to see or touch something
real (an inbox, a calendar, a shared drive, a project tracker), name
the one tool, not five.

> ___

**If the answer is "none":** that's a valid answer, and often the
right starting point. Build the skill first. Add a connection later,
once you trust the skill's output.

---

## 4. The guardrail I'd want in place before I'd trust it with that tool

Today's demo could draft a reply but never send one. What's the
equivalent boundary for your task? What should the agent always stop
and ask you before doing?

> ___

**If you're stuck:** finish this sentence — "I'd trust this completely,
except for the part where it could ___."  That's your guardrail.

---

## What happens next

Turn your answers to 1 and 2 into a filled-in copy of
`skill-starter-template.md`. Your answer to 3 tells you whether you
need `connector-primer.md` at all. Your answer to 4 becomes the one
rule you write into the skill itself — not an afterthought, the first
thing it should know about itself.
