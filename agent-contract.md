# Agent Contract — Relationship Follow-Through Agent

*Fill this in before you connect anything. Ten minutes. It becomes the
agent's instructions in whatever tool you use, and it is the reason
your agent will behave differently from the person's next to you.*

An agent with no contract guesses. This page tells it who to watch,
what to notice, what it may do, and where it must stop.

---

## 1. Who it watches

One person, or one small set of people tied to one project.

> Name / role: ___
>
> Why this relationship matters right now: ___

*Not your whole inbox. One relationship is enough for the first agent;
breadth comes later, on a schedule, once it has earned trust.*

## 2. What counts as a commitment

Write the rule the way you would explain it to a new assistant.

> A commitment from me is: ___
>
> A commitment from them is: ___
>
> These are NOT commitments (suggestions, ideas, "we should
> sometime"): ___

## 3. What it flags

Tick the ones that matter for this relationship, and add your own.

- [ ] An unanswered question, in either direction
- [ ] A decision that has stalled for more than ___ days
- [ ] A deadline inside the next ___ days
- [ ] Something I promised with a date attached
- [ ] A message from them waiting on me for more than ___ business days
- [ ] A change of tone or a risk I might be missing
- [ ] ___

## 4. What it may draft

> It may draft: ___ (a follow-up email, a meeting note, a status
> update)
>
> In this voice: ___ (paste two sentences you actually wrote to this
> person)

## 5. What always needs my approval

> Before any message leaves my name, it must: **wait for me.**
>
> It must also stop and ask before: ___

*Today's demo could draft a reply but never send one. That was not a
limitation. It was the design. Keep it.*

## 6. Scope boundary

> Email: the last ___ days (start with 30)
>
> Meetings: ___ days back, ___ days forward (start with 14 and 14)
>
> Folder or label, if the inbox is noisy: ___
>
> Other sources it may read (chat, notes): ___

## 7. My judgment rules

Add these after the first test, when you have seen what it missed,
misunderstood, or over-inferred. One or two is enough.

> Rule 1: ___
>
> Rule 2: ___

*Examples from the room: "Do not treat a suggestion as a commitment."
"Flag anything I promised with a date." "A customer waiting more than
two business days is urgent." "Do not recommend another meeting when
an email decision would work."*

---

## Where this goes

The container is different in every tool, but the seven sections map
the same way everywhere:

- **Claude:** create a Project, paste this contract into the Project
  instructions, connect Gmail and Calendar, and grant them to the
  Project.
- **ChatGPT:** a Project's custom instructions, or a Custom GPT, with
  the same seven sections. Connectors are set per plan; check yours.
- **Gemini:** a Gem. Gems cap instructions around 4,000 characters, so
  keep sections 2, 3, 5, and 7 and trim the rest first.

Once it is in place, do not re-paste this contract into every
conversation. That is the whole point of giving the agent a contract
instead of re-explaining yourself every time.
